Custom location

Custom locations use the horizon of the nearest point on a 10 km terrain grid. Near crater rims the horizon can change by tens of degrees over a few kilometres (5 km from Shackleton's rim it differs by up to 32°), so custom results there are rough. The four named sites use horizons computed at their exact positions.

About the data

Every resource we use is credited here and in RESOURCES.md.

Checking data status…

  • Sun and Earth positions: JPL Horizons System (NASA JPL). Hourly values from 2026-12-01 to 2028-12-31 (18,288 samples) for the Moon (body 301) as seen from Earth's centre: the sub-solar point, the sub-Earth point and the Earth–Moon distance. The page corrects Earth's elevation for parallax (about 0.26°).
  • Terrain horizons and elevation: LRO LOLA south-polar elevation model (LDEM_80S_80MPP_ADJ, 80 m per pixel) via NASA PGDA (Barker et al. 2023, Planetary Science Journal 4, 183, doi:10.3847/PSJ/acf3e1). For each site we trace 360 directions out to the edge of the model from a point 2 m above the ground, correct for the Moon's curvature and keep the highest terrain angle. Sites are flat spots (slope under 5° at 80 m resolution). All four were checked against the elevation model: the Shackleton, de Gerlache and Haworth points sit on their crater rims, and the Malapert point is at the top of the massif. The map's shaded relief is computed from the same model; its shadowed areas come from the permanently shadowed region map LPSR_80S_20MPP_ADJ (same PGDA product).
  • Crater names: USGS Gazetteer of Planetary Nomenclature (Shackleton, de Gerlache, Haworth).
  • Fonts: Cinzel (titles) and Inter (text and numbers) via Google Fonts (SIL Open Font License).
  • Visuals: the space background, map styling and icons are drawn in code by the team; no third-party or AI-generated images are used.
  • Hosting: GitHub Pages.
  • Use of AI: page code was drafted with an AI assistant (Claude, Anthropic) and reviewed by the team.

Sun disk visibility, planning margins and best-pick rankings are screening aids computed by this page, not official NASA ratings. Full list of resources: RESOURCES.md.

How to read this screen

Elevation is how high the Sun or Earth is above the flat horizon, in degrees. Near the south pole the Sun stays only a few degrees up.

Azimuth is the compass direction around the horizon: 0° north, 90° east, 180° south, 270° west.

Terrain horizon is the height of crater walls and ridges in each direction. The Sun or Earth is visible only when it rises above that skyline.

Clearance is elevation minus terrain horizon. Positive means visible.

Sun disk visible is the share of the Sun (about 0.27° in radius) that rises above the skyline. Once the whole disk is clear, more height adds no power. Power GO means some sunlight reaches the site.

Comm GO means Earth is above the skyline, so a direct-to-Earth link is geometrically possible.

Planning margins require the Sun or Earth to clear the skyline by extra degrees before it counts. Dashed lines in the horizon view show those limits.

Compare dates checks the same site from different start dates. Suggest best dates scans the whole period for the windows with the most time having both sunlight and an Earth link.

Crater names, centres and diameters: USGS Gazetteer of Planetary Nomenclature. Terrain and site positions: real LRO LOLA polar DEM data (NASA PGDA, Barker et al. 2023).
